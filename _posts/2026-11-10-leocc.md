## Key Idea

Traditional congestion control algorithms perform very poorly with satellite based
internet such as Starlink. This is because measurements such as latency, throughput,
and packet loss fluctuate greatly, which throws off traditional CCAs. This paper proposes
LeoCC, a congestion control algorithm that specializes in satellite networks, which leads
to many improvements.

## Critique

The paper is overall good and the algorithm's design makes sense given the constraints
of satellite networks. However, testing is limited to only three Starlink terminals. Furthermore
the algorithm needs to perform its own detection of reconfiguration events, though this is
a limitation of Starlink and not the fault of the authors.

## Connections

Like in [No Signal to Rule Them All]({{ site.baseurl }}{% post_url 2026-10-15-no-signal-to-rule %}),
this paper shows how much congestion control depends on interpreting network
measurements. However, LeoCC focuses on a setting where those measurements fluctuate
for reasons beyond congestion. This makes me wonder how well a single congestion
control algorithm can work across very different types of networks.
